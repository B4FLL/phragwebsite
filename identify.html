Is it invasive Phragmites?

Utah has two kinds of common reed that look alike. The invasive one (Phragmites australis subsp. australis) is the one we want to map and manage. The native one (subsp. americanus) is part of healthy wetlands and should be left alone. Experts often can't tell them apart from a photo alone, so if you're unsure, report it as "not sure" and a reviewer will take a look.

Invasive (subsp. australis)

  • Stems are tan or straw-colored, dull and rough with ridges.
  • Old leaf sheaths stay stuck to the stem, even in winter.
  • Grows in dense stands with almost no other plants mixed in.
  • Often taller, up to about 4–5 m (13–16 ft).
  • Seed heads are full and dense, gray to purplish.

Native (subsp. americanus)

  • Stems are smooth and shiny, often reddish-brown near the base.
  • Old leaf sheaths fall off easily, leaving bare stems.
  • Grows in smaller, looser patches mixed with other wetland plants.
  • Usually shorter, around 2–3 m (6–10 ft).
  • Seed heads are sparser.

Stem color and leaf sheaths are the easiest clues and they are not perfect. A positive ID can require a close look at the plant's tiny parts or a DNA test.

Please don't cut or pull Phragmites yourself. Disturbing it can spread it. Report it on the map and contact your county weed control office.
