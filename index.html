Taking on invasive Phragmites

Invasive Phragmites is a tall reed that takes over wetlands and shorelines, crowding out native plants and the animals that depend on them. This is how our team is working on the problem.

Explore the Utah map

The Problem

Phragmites australis, also called common reed, has a non-native strain that spreads quickly across North American wetlands. It can grow more than 4 meters (15 feet) tall and forms dense stands that block sunlight and push out native species.

Spreads fast

It spreads through seeds and long underground stems (rhizomes), so one patch can quickly become many.

Hurts habitat

Thick stands replace the mix of native plants that birds, fish, and insects need for food and shelter.

Hard to remove

Cutting it down isn't enough — the roots survive and regrow, so control takes years of effort.
